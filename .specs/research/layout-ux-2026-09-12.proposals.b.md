# Explorer B proposals — Layout panel UI/UX (interaction-model / intent-semantic leans)

Context: `.specs/research/layout-ux-context.md`. NOTE: generated in-session after subagent quota failures; independence degraded vs the ToT ideal.

---

## B1 — Intent sliders as dimensionless multipliers (Spread / Stiffness / Settle)

**Summary:** The primary controls become 2–4 semantic sliders phrased as outcomes, each a multiplier on the resolved base (data + regime), write-through into the existing options struct — no engine change.

Users think in outcomes, not constants. Spread (compact ↔ sparse) jointly scales repulsion strength and rest lengths, preserving their ratio — for a molecule it scales the UFF geometry, for a vault the spring_len/repulsion pair. Stiffness (loose ↔ stiff) scales spring_k — how strongly edges win over repulsion. Settle (faster ↔ smoother) packages cooling rate, damping, and the energy-halt threshold into one direction of travel. Each slider shows its multiplier (×0.5 … ×2.0, neutral 1.0) and composes with typed data because it multiplies whatever the base resolved to, including per-edge UFF values — it cannot collide with data the way absolute constants do. Constants remain under an Advanced disclosure for power users.

Key decisions: (1) multipliers, not absolutes — the unit-mismatch class of bug becomes unrepresentable; (2) few intents, each with a real, testable effect across all six use cases; (3) write-through mapping (intent → option fields) is a small per-regime table, configuration not code. Trade-offs: intents are lossy projections of the constant space (some constant combinations unreachable); naming the intents well is hard ("Spread" must mean the same thing at 24 nodes and 100k); remote engines with non-force settings schemas need per-engine intent mappings or the sliders gray out. **Probability 0.82. Complexity medium.** Risks: mushy feel if mappings aren't tuned per regime; users who want one specific constant resent the indirection; multiplier persistence semantics across regime switches (does ×1.3 Spread survive a regime change? — answer must be designed).

## B2 — Direct manipulation on the canvas (the graph is the UI)

**Summary:** Drag a cluster to increase local spread, pinch the canvas to scale bond lengths, scroll on empty space to tune gravity — the panel reflects the derived values, mostly read-only.

The most radical answer to "the sliders lie": remove the abstraction. The force sim already supports cursor forces (`cursor_pos/radius/strength`); extending that into persistent, named interactions turns layout tuning into spatial play. A "bond scale" gesture scales all rest lengths (typed or not) around the centroid; a "spread" gesture injects a temporary repulsion burst and records the resulting equilibrium as the new target. The panel becomes a readout of what the sim is doing plus lock toggles ("hold this cluster").

Key decisions: (1) gestures produce *persistent parameter changes*, not just transient forces — otherwise it's a toy; (2) panel as reflection, not control surface; (3) every gesture has an undo (the sim's wake/solve cycle already implies recomputation). Trade-offs: gesture discoverability is the classic direct-manipulation cost; precision editing (exact spring_k) is worse than a slider; conflicts with existing canvas interactions (camera orbit, node drag, lasso) in an already gesture-dense surface; significant renderer/input work in `graph_canvas.rs`. **Probability 0.08. Complexity high.** Risks: gesture collision with camera controls; non-obvious mapping from gesture to parameter; WASM pointer-event plumbing is real work; accessibility (keyboard-only users locked out without a parallel control path).

## B3 — Live regime comparison (A/B/C preview from the actual graph)

**Summary:** Show 2–3 small live previews of the *actual loaded graph* relaxing under candidate regimes side by side; clicking one applies that regime to the main canvas.

Choosing a regime from names ("Balanced"?) is guesswork; choosing from outcomes is recognition. The panel renders thumbnails of the real graph (subsampled at high n) running 1–2 seconds of relaxation under each candidate regime: `Vault · 10.2k`, `Molecular · UFF`, `Pretty`. Differences in regime stop being abstractions — the user sees the molecule collapse vs hold its geometry, the vault spread vs clump. The chosen regime applies to the main canvas with one click. Previews reuse the existing WASM layout path (graph-layouts runs in-process; a 200-node subsample relaxes in milliseconds).

Key decisions: (1) recognition over recall for regime selection; (2) previews are of *this* graph, not stock imagery — honesty about what the regime does to *your* data; (3) subsampling for large graphs (preview fidelity vs cost trade-off stated in the UI). Trade-offs: panel real estate (thumbnails in 340–480px); compute cost of parallel preview sims (bounded by subsampling); preview↔full divergence at high n (the subsample may look better than the full run). **Probability 0.06. Complexity high.** Risks: preview/full-layout divergence misleads; multi-sim resource contention on the WASM main thread; thumbnail rendering pipeline is new surface in the wgpu renderer.

## B4 — Goal-based control (declare outcomes, sim tunes itself)

**Summary:** Replace parameter editing with goal declarations — "communities separated", "bonds within 10% of UFF targets", "settle in under 5s" — and let the sim search its own parameters; the panel shows goals and their measured satisfaction.

The deepest version of intent-driven UI: the user never names a parameter at all. Goals map to measurable sim telemetry the codebase already produces (per-edge rest error vs UFF — exactly what the scenario gates measure; KE/energy-halt state; community modularity from graph-metrics). A small controller adjusts repulsion/spring scale/cooling against the goals (a bounded hill-climb over 2–3 dimensions, run client-side). The panel becomes a goal checklist with live satisfaction meters and an overall "converged / searching / stuck" state.

Key decisions: (1) goals are measurable or they don't ship as goals; (2) the search space is tiny and bounded (2–3 dimensions, capped iterations) — not an AutoML framework; (3) manual constants remain as an escape hatch. Trade-offs: controller design is genuinely hard (local optima, oscillation); goal vocabulary must cover vault aesthetics ("communities separated" is fuzzy to measure); users lose direct causality ("why did it choose repulsion 900?"). **Probability 0.05. Complexity high.** Risks: controller instability misattributed to the engine; fuzzy goals need new metrics; "stuck" UX (what does the user do when a goal can't be satisfied?) is an unsolved design problem in itself.

## B5 — Scrub the relaxation timeline (pause at the frame you like, keep it)

**Summary:** The relaxation becomes a scrubbable timeline — drag back to the frame where the layout looked right, "keep this state" freezes positions; the Timeline panel (already existing) becomes the layout UX for live sims.

Force layouts pass through visually distinct phases (untangle → communities form → settle). Users already think "stop, that was better two seconds ago". The Timeline panel records position snapshots during relaxation; scrubbing replays them on the main canvas; "keep" writes the positions as the new authored state (frozen, sim paused). Parameters become secondary: the user picked an *outcome frame* directly. Cooling/energy-halt are reframed as "how fast the timeline runs".

Key decisions: (1) reuse the existing Timeline panel and its frame capture rather than new surface; (2) "keep" = freeze positions + pause, which the engine already supports (halt/pause paths exist); (3) snapshot ring bounded (memory at 100k nodes: positions only, f32×3, e.g. 60 frames). Trade-offs: doesn't help initial regime choice (only post-hoc frame picking); frozen positions are brittle across graph reloads (reload re-seeds); storage/bandwidth at 100k nodes needs care. **Probability 0.07. Complexity medium.** Risks: snapshot memory bounds; "keep" semantics across reload/re-import unclear; timeline replay on the wgpu canvas is new plumbing (positions are double-buffered for the sim).

## B6 — One master dial (Quality ↔ Speed) + auto-everything-else

**Summary:** Collapse the entire settings surface into a single dial that trades layout quality for convergence speed; everything else auto-resolves from graph size, class, and typed data.

The maximal-distillation answer: one dial, one regime capsule, one run state. The dial maps onto a curated path through parameter space (steps_per_call, cooling rate, repulsion_samples/theta for the backend, energy-halt threshold) — the same trade-off the presets already embody, but continuous instead of three named points. Auto-resolution does the rest: for_n_nodes for scale, typed-data detection for molecular regime, backend choice by node count (exact/BH/NS thresholds). The philosophy: the panel's job is to be right by default, not to be adjustable.

Key decisions: (1) the dial is continuous but its path through parameter space is a curated, versioned table (config, not code); (2) everything auto-resolved is *shown* (regime capsule) so the automation is legible, not magic; (3) Advanced disclosure survives for the 5% who need a specific constant. Trade-offs: a single path can't fit both vault and molecular aesthetics perfectly (the dial means slightly different things per regime); power users lose orthogonal control (quality and speed are not always opposed); presets Fast/Balanced/Pretty become dial positions (migration of persisted state needed). **Probability 0.80. Complexity low.** Risks: dial-path tuning is subjective and will be relitigated; one-dimensional control frustrates when quality AND speed both matter (big graph on weak GPU); auto-resolution misfires are more damaging when there's no visible knob to correct them.
